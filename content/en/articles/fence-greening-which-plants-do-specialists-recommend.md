# Fence Greening – Which Plants Do Specialists Recommend?

Fences are often chosen for privacy, security or to mark the boundaries of a property. Once the fence is installed, the natural next step is **landscaping the yard**.  
Well-chosen plants can make the yard cosier, hide technical installations (such as wells) and help create a harmonious environment.

To make the decision easier, here are the plants specialists recommend and the key principles of fence greening.

<!--more-->

---

## Which Plants to Choose for Fence Greening?

When choosing plants, consider:

- whether you want annual or perennial plants,
- how much care you can give them,
- what effect you are after – privacy, colour, greenery or decoration,
- which way the fence faces (sunny or shady spot).

Annual plants let you experiment, while climbing plants create a dense green backdrop.

---

## Recommended Plants for Fence Greening

### **Hyacinth bean (Lablab)**

A decorative, fragrant plant with small purple flowers.  
Features:

- loves warmth and sun,
- grows best in light, fertile soil,
- flowers until autumn,
- suitable for bouquets and decoration.

A great choice if you want to add colour and scent.

---

### **Vigna**

An annual climbing plant that can grow up to **7 metres**.

- flowers with rich violet blooms,
- likes a sunny spot,
- grows in loose, light soil,
- decorative and quick to cover surfaces.

Suitable if you want fast and dense greening.

---

### **Clematis**

A perennial climbing plant with large, bright flowers – from white to violet and red.

- overwinters well in Lithuania,
- likes its roots in the shade and its leaves and flowers in the sun,
- needs a support to cling to with its leaf stalks,
- depending on the variety, flowers from early summer to autumn.

An exceptional choice that gives the fence a luxurious look.

---

### **Climbing honeysuckle**

A plant known for:

- lush foliage,
- a strong scent,
- fast growth.

A great fit if you want more privacy and natural greenery.

---

### **Virginia creeper**

One of the most versatile climbing plants.

- can creep along the ground or climb upwards,
- hides unsightly spots very well,
- decorative all season thanks to its lush foliage (the flowers are small and inconspicuous),
- turns bright colours in autumn,
- grows slowly in the first year, then very quickly.

An ideal choice for larger areas and a natural look.

---

## Fence Greening Methods

For plants to grow healthy and look good for a long time, follow a few rules.

### **1. Climbing plants need supports**

Plants should not lean on the fence itself (especially a metal one), so you need to:

- install wooden supports,
- stretch twine or rope,
- provide enough supports.

Young plants grow upwards and later attach themselves to surfaces.

---

### **2. Consider which way the fence faces**

- **North side** – choose frost-hardy plants (e.g. honeysuckle, ivy).
- **South side** – sun-loving plants that flower abundantly are a good fit.

The right spot determines the plant's health and how abundantly it flowers.

---

## Conclusion

Fence greening is a great way to make your yard cosier, more colourful and more natural.  
By choosing the right plants and following the greening methods, you will create a long-lasting, attractive result that you can enjoy all year round.
